A home for the nonprofits that serve Nashville

The Honey Alexander Center houses only nonprofit organizations, each working to support children, families and communities across Nashville and greater Tennessee.

2400 Clifton Ave · Nashville, TN

Who's in the building

Two organizations call the center home today. Reach each one directly for services, volunteering and giving.

Family & Children's ServiceNashville nonprofit since 1943

“Our mission is to connect individuals and families to hope, to healing, and to one another.”
Family & Children's Service

F&CS serves people in crisis and transition by meeting them where they are, understanding their needs and connecting them to the resources they need. It often fills the gaps in social services, a safety net so that all children and families can be safe and healthy.

Founded
1943, by volunteers finding homes for children orphaned in WWII
Reach
More than 90,000 Tennesseans served each year
  • Mental health & counseling
  • Child & family permanency
  • Economic stability
  • Support for public education

Retrieving IndependenceService dogs, in the center since 2021

“To increase independence and restore dignity through the training and placement of working dogs.”
Retrieving Independence mission

Founded in Franklin in 2012 by Dr. Brenda Dew and Lesley Adams, Retrieving Independence trains and places service dogs, facility dogs for schools and professional settings, and skilled companions for in-home support. Much of the training happens through Serving with Canines, its prison-based program where incarcerated trainers prepare dogs for placement.

Graduates
More than 100 working dogs placed since 2012
Training
Three prison-based programs and 80+ active volunteers

Named for Honey Alexander

Dedicated June 2019

The Honey Alexander Center is named in honor of F&CS benefactor and former Tennessee First Lady Honey Alexander.

Honey was a powerful advocate for children and families during and after her time as First Lady. She was a longtime volunteer, board member and chair, and generous supporter of Family & Children's Service. The center was officially dedicated in her honor in June 2019.
